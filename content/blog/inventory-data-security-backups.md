Inventory data includes product information, prices, supplier records, sales, purchasing, staff activity, and sometimes customer details. Losing access to that information or allowing unauthorised changes can disrupt operations and damage trust.

## Start with access control

Give each person the access needed for their role. A cashier does not need the same permissions as a purchasing manager, and a temporary worker should not keep access after leaving. Review accounts and permissions regularly, especially after staff changes.

## Protect the important actions

Creating products, changing prices, approving purchases, adjusting stock, and processing returns can affect cash and inventory. Require appropriate permissions and retain an audit history. A record of who changed something is useful for training and investigation, not only for security incidents.

## Backups need a recovery test

Ask what is backed up, how often, where it is stored, how long it is retained, and who can restore it. A backup that has never been tested is an assumption. Define a recovery procedure and confirm that the business can access the information needed to continue operating.

## Secure devices and sessions

Use strong unique passwords, protect shared terminals, sign out of public devices, and keep operating systems and browsers updated. Avoid putting passwords in spreadsheets or sharing one account among many people; shared credentials remove accountability.

## Ask a software vendor

Before selecting inventory software, ask about encryption, backups, access controls, audit logs, data export, incident response, and account recovery. Also ask how offline transactions are protected and reconciled when connectivity returns.

## Protect against everyday mistakes

Security is not only about sophisticated attacks. A shared password, an unattended terminal, an accidental bulk edit, or a staff account that remains active after departure can create a serious operational problem. Use simple controls consistently: individual accounts, least-privilege access, session protection, approval for high-impact actions, and regular review of users.

## Make continuity practical

Write down who should be contacted if a user loses access, a device is lost, a report looks wrong, or the business suspects an unauthorised change. Keep the procedure accessible to the people responsible for operations. Test a sample data export and confirm that the business understands what it contains.

## Questions for an owner to ask

- Can we export our products and transactions in a usable format?
- Can we see who changed a stock quantity or price?
- Can we remove a former staff member immediately?
- What happens to pending offline transactions?
- How often are backups tested, not only created?

Good security supports the business without making ordinary work impossible. Clear roles and traceable actions usually help both security and operational discipline.
