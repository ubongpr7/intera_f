When a shop depends on an internet connection for every sale, an outage can become a business interruption. Customers still want to buy, staff still need to record transactions, and managers still need confidence that the stock record will reconcile later.

## What offline should mean

Offline capability is not simply showing an old screen. A useful offline workflow should make clear which actions can be completed without a connection, keep a local queue or record, and synchronise safely when connectivity returns. Teams also need a visible way to identify unresolved sync issues.

## Questions to ask before choosing a platform

- Can a cashier complete the core sale workflow offline?
- How are duplicate or conflicting changes handled?
- Can managers see which transactions are pending synchronisation?
- Does the system protect local data on shared devices?
- What reports remain available during an outage?

Test these scenarios with real devices and realistic product data. A vendor should explain the recovery path, not only demonstrate a successful sale on a connected network.

## Keep the process simple

Offline support works best when the product catalogue, prices, permissions, and staff workflow are prepared ahead of time. Train the team to reconcile the pending transactions after the connection returns and to avoid creating a second manual record unless the process explicitly requires it.

## Test the recovery path, not only the sale

During a pilot, disconnect a test device and complete several permitted transactions. Reconnect it and observe how the system synchronises. Check whether the transactions appear once, whether stock changes are applied in the right order, and whether a manager can identify anything still pending. Test a return, a cancelled order, a price change, and a shared product updated from another device.

## Decide what should remain available offline

The safest offline scope is usually the core selling workflow and the minimum product, price, permission, and tax information required for it. High-risk administration, large catalogue edits, and reports that depend on current central data may require a connection. The product should make those boundaries clear to staff.

## Protect devices and local data

Use individual staff access where practical, lock shared terminals, remove departing staff promptly, and define what happens if a device is lost. Offline support should improve continuity without creating an uncontrolled copy of the business record.

## Write a short outage procedure

Tell the team how to continue selling, how to identify a pending transaction, who approves a manual fallback, and how reconciliation is completed. Practise the procedure before a real outage. The best technology still needs a process that people understand under pressure.
